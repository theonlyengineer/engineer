Privacy Policy

Last updated 2026-11-07

This policy covers every mobile application published by THE ONLY ENGINEER. Each app also has its own section below stating exactly what that app does and does not collect. Where the two differ, the app's own section is the accurate one.

The short version. Our apps are built to work without you creating an account. Some of them collect nothing at all and cannot — they have no permission to reach the internet. Apps that show ads or offer subscriptions necessarily involve Google, and an app with online play keeps a small record of its players on Google's Firebase service. What that means is set out below, per app, rather than in general terms that would be true of nothing.

1. Who we are

THE ONLY ENGINEER ("we", "us") publishes the mobile applications listed in section 4. We are based in India. For anything in this policy, write to nizar@theonlyengineer.com.

2. What this policy is, and is not

This policy describes how our apps handle information. It does not describe how Google handles information — Google Play and Google AdMob are operated by Google and are governed by Google's Privacy Policy. Where one of our apps uses a Google service, we say so and we link there; we cannot speak for what Google does with what it receives.

3. What we never do

  • We do not sell personal information, and we do not share it with data brokers.
  • We do not ask for your real name, email address, phone number or contacts. An app with online play asks for a display name, which can be anything you like.
  • We do not require you to sign up for an account to use any of our apps. Where an app has online play, it creates an anonymous guest account when you open it, and signing in with Google is optional.
  • We do not track you across other companies' apps or websites ourselves. Where one of our apps shows ads, Google may use your advertising ID to choose them; that app's section in section 4 says so, and how to limit it.
  • We do not knowingly collect anything from children under 13. None of our apps is directed at children, and none participates in Google Play's Families programme.

4. Per-app detail

This is the part that matters. Anything general above is subordinate to what is written here.

Hushcoil

Hushcoil sends information off your phone for two things only: ads, and crash reports. The game itself — every level, the daily knot, your hints and your progress — works offline and stays on your device. There is no account, and the app contains no analytics of its own.

Ads, through Google AdMob. Hushcoil shows full-screen ads after some levels and reward videos you choose to watch. To request and show them, Google's ad software inside the app collects the following and sends it to Google:

  • your approximate location, estimated from your IP address;
  • how you use the app, such as app launches, taps and video views;
  • diagnostics, such as app launch time, hangs and energy use;
  • device identifiers: your phone's advertising ID and app set ID.

This is used for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention, and it is shared with Google, which serves the ads. It is encrypted in transit. What Google does with it is governed by Google's Privacy Policy and explained in how Google uses information from apps that use its services. We see only totals, such as how many ads were shown. We do not receive your data in a form that identifies you, and we cannot look up or delete one person's records.

Crash reports, through Google Firebase Crashlytics. If Hushcoil crashes, it sends a crash report: what the app was doing when it crashed, your phone's model and Android version, and a random ID for this installation, which is not your advertising ID. Google processes it for us, and we use it only to find and fix crashes, never for advertising. Crash reports are encrypted in transit and deleted after 90 days. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland none is sent unless you agree, and where you are asked, Privacy options lets you change your answer.

Your consent and your choices. Before any ad is requested, Hushcoil asks Google's User Messaging Platform whether a consent message applies where you are, such as the GDPR message in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland or a US state privacy message, and shows it if one does. Where that applies, Privacy options in Hushcoil's settings lets you change your choice at any time. On any Android phone you can also reset or delete your advertising ID in Android's settings (under Privacy → Ads or Google → Ads, depending on the phone).

No purchases. Hushcoil sells nothing: there are no in-app purchases, so we never see any payment details.

Your progress. Your level, hints, daily-knot streak, settings and any board in progress are kept in one file in the app's private storage on your phone. If Android backup is on for your Google account, Android includes that file in your device backup so your progress can move to a new phone. That backup is held in your own Google account, not by us. Uninstalling the app removes the file from your phone.

Ludo Jaldi: Play with Friends

Playing on one phone, or against the computer, keeps everything on your device. Your game in progress, your settings and your counts of games played and won are kept in the app's private storage. Two things send information off your phone: the ads, and online play if you choose it.

Ads, through Google AdMob. Ludo Jaldi shows a banner on its home and game screens and a full-screen ad when a game ends. To request and show them, Google's ad software inside the app collects the following and sends it to Google:

  • your approximate location, estimated from your IP address;
  • how you use the app, such as app launches, taps and video views;
  • diagnostics, such as app launch time, hangs and energy use;
  • device identifiers: your phone's advertising ID and app set ID.

This is used for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention, and it is shared with Google, which serves the ads. It is encrypted in transit. What Google does with it is governed by Google's Privacy Policy and explained in how Google uses information from apps that use its services. We see only totals, such as how many ads were shown.

Your consent and your choices. Before any ad is requested, Ludo Jaldi asks Google's User Messaging Platform whether a consent message applies where you are, such as the GDPR message in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland or a US state privacy message, and shows it if one does. Where that applies, Privacy options in Settings lets you change your choice at any time. On any Android phone you can also reset or delete your advertising ID in Android's settings.

Online play (optional), through Google Firebase. If you open Play online, the app creates an anonymous guest account with Firebase Authentication, a service run by Google. You may also sign in with Google, which links that account to your Google account; Firebase then holds that account's email address and name as part of the sign-in. We keep the following on Firebase so a game can be shared between phones:

  • a player record: the display name you chose (up to 12 characters), when you were last seen online and when it was created;
  • game rooms: the room code, who sits where and under what display name, the game's state and every move, emoji reactions, and when each player was last active;
  • reports: if you report another player's display name, we keep your anonymous account identifier, the reported player's identifier and display name, the room code and the time, so that we can review it. Reports are visible only to us, never to other players;
  • the account identifier Firebase assigns, and whatever Google's service logs, such as your IP address, when your phone connects to it.

Other players in a room can see your display name, your moves and your reactions. A room is open to anyone with its code, and a public quick-match room can be joined by any player. A room is marked to expire a day after it was last used; removal of expired rooms is done by the app when someone opens online play, so a finished room can remain a little longer. We do not use this information for advertising, and we do not share it with anyone but Google, which runs the service for us. A name that breaks our rules (see the Terms of Service) can be reported in the room or from the pause menu; we review reports, act on them, and delete a report once it has been dealt with.

Deleting it. In Ludo Jaldi, open Settings, then Online, then Delete my online profile. That removes your player record and your sign-in account. See Data and account deletion for the details, and for what to do if the app cannot complete it.

5. Your rights

Because we keep none of your information ourselves, you exercise most rights directly. Our apps hold your information on your own device, and uninstalling the app removes it. Where an app shows ads, the ad data goes to Google rather than to us, and that app's section in section 4 says how to control it. If you believe we hold anything about you, you may ask us to confirm what we hold, to correct it, to delete it, or to stop processing it, by writing to nizar@theonlyengineer.com. We will respond within 30 days.

See also Data and account deletion.

6. Security

Data held on your device is protected by your device: Android's application sandbox keeps our apps' files private to those apps. We run no servers of our own. For the apps listed in section 4, the only information held away from your device is ad data held by Google, and, for Ludo Jaldi's online play, the records described above, which sit on Google's Firebase service and are protected by Google's security and by access rules that let a player change only their own record.

7. Changes to this policy

If an app starts collecting something it did not collect before, this page is updated before that version is published, and the "last updated" date at the top changes. We do not make a change retroactive. Previous versions are available on request.
